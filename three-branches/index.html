The Three Branches of the U.S. Government

The U.S. Constitution divides power among three co-equal branches so that no single part of government can dominate. This is the principle of the separation of powers.

Explore the branches Test yourself
🏛

Legislative Branch

Makes the laws

Established by Article I, the legislative branch is the Congress, a bicameral body made up of the House of Representatives and the Senate.

  • Who Congress (House + Senate)
  • Members 435 Representatives, 100 Senators
  • Key powers Pass laws, declare war, tax & spend, confirm appointments
🏢

Executive Branch

Enforces the laws

Established by Article II, the executive branch is led by the President, who serves as head of state, head of government, and commander in chief.

  • Who President, Vice President, Cabinet
  • Term 4 years (max two terms)
  • Key powers Sign or veto bills, command the military, conduct foreign policy
⚖

Judicial Branch

Interprets the laws

Established by Article III, the judicial branch is the Supreme Court and the federal courts, which apply and interpret the law in individual cases.

  • Who Supreme Court + federal courts
  • Justices 9, appointed for life
  • Key powers Interpret laws, judicial review, resolve disputes

Checks & Balances

Each branch can limit the powers of the others, keeping government in balance.

Congress checks the President

Overrides vetoes, controls funding, and can impeach and remove the President.

Congress checks the Courts

Confirms or rejects judges and can propose constitutional amendments.

President checks Congress

Can veto legislation passed by Congress.

President checks the Courts

Nominates federal judges and Supreme Court justices.

Courts check Congress

Can declare laws unconstitutional through judicial review.

Courts check the President

Can rule executive actions unconstitutional.
