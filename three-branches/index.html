One Government,
Three Branches

The U.S. Constitution divides federal power among three separate branches — so that no single person or group can control the government alone. This is called the separation of powers.

🏛️ Legislative Makes the laws 🦅 Executive Enforces the laws ⚖️ Judicial Interprets the laws

Scroll to explore each branch ↓

Article I of the Constitution

01 The Legislative Branch

Makes the laws — the United States Congress

Congress is bicameral, meaning it has two chambers: the Senate and the House of Representatives. A bill must pass both chambers before it can go to the President to be signed into law.

Senate

100

senators — 2 per state, serving 6-year terms

House

435

representatives, apportioned by state population, serving 2-year terms

Key Powers

  • Write and pass federal laws
  • Declare war and fund the military
  • Levy taxes and control federal spending
  • Confirm presidential appointments (Senate)
  • Impeach and remove federal officials

Article II of the Constitution

02 The Executive Branch

Enforces the laws — the President of the United States

The President is both head of state and Commander in Chief of the armed forces. The executive branch also includes the Vice President, the Cabinet, and the federal agencies that carry out the laws Congress passes.

Term

4

years per term, limited to two elected terms by the 22nd Amendment

Cabinet

15

executive departments, from State and Defense to Education

Key Powers

  • Sign bills into law — or veto them
  • Command the armed forces
  • Negotiate treaties with other nations
  • Appoint federal judges and ambassadors
  • Grant pardons for federal offenses

Article III of the Constitution

03 The Judicial Branch

Interprets the laws — the Supreme Court and federal courts

The courts decide what laws mean and whether they follow the Constitution. Since Marbury v. Madison (1803), the Supreme Court has held the power of judicial review — the authority to strike down laws and executive actions that violate the Constitution.

Justices

9

Supreme Court justices, appointed for life

Circuits

13

federal courts of appeals below the Supreme Court

Key Powers

  • Interpret the meaning of laws
  • Declare laws or executive acts unconstitutional
  • Resolve disputes between states
  • Hear appeals from lower courts
  • Serve for life to stay independent of politics

Checks & Balances

Each branch can limit the power of the other two. Select a branch to see how it keeps the others in check.
