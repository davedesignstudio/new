# Design research — 1950 to now

Labeled shelves. Parallel streams — not a civilizational ladder.  
**Action:** Surveyed · **Item:** graphic / identity / industrial / interface / liberation print / fashion / architecture / type · **Limit:** synthesis for studio craft, not a complete encyclopedia of every school, nation, or decade.

## How to read this

- Shelves run **in parallel** where dates overlap. Swiss grid and Black Panther newspaper graphics share decades; they are not rungs on one ladder.
- Each shelf names **what it gave the field** and a **limit** (what it cannot claim).
- Studio mapping sits at the end — how this history feeds D Philhower Studio craft, the Service the Hills subdomain, identity kits, and the Techniques canon.

## Shelf A — 1950–1970 · Order as craft

### Swiss / International Typographic Style
- Grid, sans-serif (Akzidenz lineage → Helvetica / Univers, both 1957), photography over ornament, objective tone.
- Centres: Zurich (*Neue Grafik*; Josef Müller-Brockmann) and Basel (Armin Hofmann, Emil Ruder); Yale link spreads the method into the US.
- **Gave us:** modular layout as default for corporate and public information design.
- **Limit:** “neutrality” claimed as universal; later critics named the politics of “no politics.”

### Mid-century modern (objects + rooms)
- Clean lines, new materials (plywood, plastics, tubular steel), democratic comfort.
- Continuity from Bauhaus diaspora into US / European industry (Knoll, Eames, Herman Miller; Braun / Dieter Rams).
- Scandinavian warm modernism (Denmark, Sweden, Finland) as a parallel comfort tradition — wood, light, domestic scale.
- **Gave us:** “form follows function” as consumer-goods ethics — good objects for daily use.
- **Limit:** mass taste later flattened “mid-century” into a lifestyle sticker.

### Corporate identity systems (US + global firms)
- Logo → **system** (stationery, fleet, signage, packaging, film).
- Figures: Paul Rand (IBM, ABC, UPS); Saul Bass (AT&T/Bell, United; film titles as prologue, not decoration); Otl Aicher (Munich Olympics 1972 pictograms — systems thinking at city scale).
- **Gave us:** brand as *operating kit*, not a single mark — ancestor of today’s logo / poster / tee / van boards.
- **Limit:** systems can erase local voice if the kit is only templates.

### Japanese industrial + graphic modernism (parallel)
- Postwar industrial recovery; graphic clarity (Yusaku Kamekura Olympic work; later MUJI / “no-brand” restraint).
- **Gave us:** restraint as luxury; empty space as structure.
- **Limit:** “minimal Japan” as Western stereotype — real practice is broader.

### Liberation + tricontinental print (parallel shelf — same decades)
- Emory Douglas / Black Panther newspaper: thick line, high contrast, block type, photomontage — design as hear+do.
- OSPAAAL / Tricontinental posters (Cuba): limited ink, coded symbols, anti-imperial graphics across Africa / Asia / Latin America.
- Black Arts / Black Power covers: slab serifs, militant clarity — not “primitive,” not a rung below Swiss; a different brief.
- **Gave us:** objecthood of the poster as weapon and newspaper; Afrofuturist futures later inherit this toolkit.
- **Shelf label:** Meqabyan ≠ Maccabees logic — name the tradition; do not collapse it into “Western modernism’s colorful cousin.”

### Pop, psychedelia, protest (late 60s overlap)
- Warhol / Pop: brand as subject. Psychedelic posters (Fillmore, Wes Wilson): illegible type as experience.
- Polish School of Posters: metaphor, craft illustration under constraint.
- **Gave us:** culture as content; poster as event.
- **Limit:** style tourism without stakes.

## Shelf B — 1968–1990 · Break the grid, keep the tools

### Postmodern / New Wave
- Critique of International Style’s cold consumer clarity after 1968.
- Wolfgang Weingart (Basel): rule-breaking type inside Swiss bones; Odermatt & Tissi: play with precision.
- US: Cranbrook (McCoy), California New Wave; later David Carson / *Ray Gun* — illegibility as attitude.
- Architecture twin: Venturi / Scott Brown (*Learning from Las Vegas*), Memphis Group furniture — signs and surface as meaning.
- **Gave us:** permission for voice, collage, irony — and a warning that chaos without stakes is just noise.
- **Limit:** pastiche that quotes without pressure-testing use.

### Dutch / UK / Italian currents
- Total Design (NL); British independent magazines and album covers; Italian radical design (Archizoom, Memphis) as furniture/graphics crossover.
- **Gave us:** national schools as labeled shelves, not one “Western” blob.

### Tech shift under the aesthetic war
- Phototypesetting → cheaper, faster experiments.
- Xerox PARC → WIMP (windows, icons, menus, pointer); early GUIs teach metaphor.
- 1984 Macintosh + PostScript: desktop publishing; Adobe Illustrator (1987), Photoshop (1990), Fontographer → indie type foundries.
- **Gave us:** design labor moves from paste-up to file; craft becomes pressure-test of systems, not only hand polish.
- **Limit:** “anyone can design” without training — volume without craft.

### Fashion as mass image (brief)
- Ready-to-wear globalization; logo as street signal (Nike swoosh era solidifies).
- **Gave us:** brand mark as wearable media — tee / van kits sit here.

## Shelf C — 1990–2010 · Screen as substrate

### Early web → Web 2.0
- Tables, GIF culture, Flash — then CSS, web fonts, CMS, SEO as layout constraint.
- Branding expands to sites, apps, social avatars — identity must *reflow*.
- Flash era motion → then HTML5 / CSS transitions as craft.
- **Gave us:** responsive thinking; design that survives unknown viewports.
- **Limit:** trend CSS that ages into sludge.

### Branding boom + anti-brand
- Global identity manuals thicken; meanwhile “no logo” critique (Klein) and DIY zine culture run parallel.
- **Gave us:** brand ethics as a brief item, not only aesthetics.

### Skeuomorphism → Flat (late shelf into 2010s)
- iOS / early apps mimic physical materials (leather, felt, gloss) to teach touch.
- ~2011–2013: Flat Design strips chrome (Metro / Windows 8; iOS 7); “authentically digital.”
- **Gave us:** clarity at small sizes; risk of generic sameness and weak affordances.
- **Limit:** flat without signifiers fails discoverability (NN/g Flat 2.0 critique).

### Design systems harden
- Style guides → living component libraries → design tokens.
- **Gave us:** Brand OS thinking — change once, deploy everywhere (studio GenUI / envelope contract lineage).
- **Limit:** systems that forbid local exception become bureaucracy.

### Accessibility enters the brief (ongoing from here)
- WCAG matures; contrast, keyboard, captions become craft requirements, not polish.
- **Gave us:** pressure-test language for UI and print legibility (van / tee distance).

## Shelf D — 2010–now · Systems, motion, generation

### Material Design and platform grammars
- Google Material (2014): metaphor + motion + elevation as shared language; later Material You / expressive variants.
- Apple HIG, Fluent, Carbon, etc. — platform grammars designers speak.
- Soft UI / neo-skeuomorphism as a Flat 2.0 compromise (depth without leather kitsch).
- **Gave us:** interaction as craft (duration, easing, hierarchy), not only static layout.

### Variable type, motion brands, adaptive logos
- Variable fonts; responsive logo tiers (full lockup → icon → favicon).
- Motion identity (logo that breathes on screen); sonic branding as twin.
- **Gave us:** STH-style *text systems* that must also work as pixels and foil.
- **Limit:** motion that entertains without hierarchy.

### Social, content, and “template culture”
- Instagram grids, Stories, TikTok vertical — composition for feed velocity.
- Canva / template economies: democratization + visual sameness.
- **Gave us:** speed of production; **Limit:** AI-cliché stacks (purple gradients, cream+terracotta, broadsheet cosplay) without a brief.

### Sustainability + circular design
- Material honesty, repairability, packaging reduction — design ethics as constraint.
- **Gave us:** Limit as craft (what we will not print / ship).

### Inclusive + decolonial practice
- Broader authorship; critique of Eurocentric canons; Indigenous, Global South, and diaspora studios named on their own shelves.
- Afrofuturist craft (ongoing, not a late add-on): speculative, technical, ancestral, *made* — Sun Ra → contemporary Black design / tech / fashion / graphics.
- **Studio art rule:** objecthood + Afrofuturist craft; no civilizational hierarchy aesthetics.
- **Shelf label:** parallel crafts, labeled — Meqabyan ≠ Maccabees.

### Generative / GenUI (current edge)
- AI-assisted layout, image, and copy; streaming partial UIs; agent-built interfaces.
- **Craft rule (studio canon):** envelope contract before stream; partial-safe status as Action + Item + Limit; front-load stakes (legal, danger, cost) — never stage them away.
- **Pressure-test ≠ polish:** stress the joint (accessibility, rights, truth claims) before shine.
- **Limit:** generation without a brief is paste; generation without citation is theft-adjacent.

## How this maps to studio work now

1. **Caps–script wordmark system** (caps serif + script + tagline) sits on the corporate-identity shelf (Bass/Rand systems) + mid-century craft of hierarchy.
2. **Logo / poster / tee / van kits** = identity as operating kit, not a lone mark — Shelf A corporate + Shelf B fashion-as-media.
3. **Techniques canon** (`DESIGN-TECHNIQUES.md`) = 2010s–now response: GenUI contracts, front-loaded stakes, objecthood, plain writing, labeled canons, cooperage pressure-test.
4. **Liberation print shelf** remains open — hear+do (Mt 7), not quote-only modernism.
5. **Avoid AI default looks** = refuse template culture’s purple/cream/broadsheet clusters unless the brief earns them.
