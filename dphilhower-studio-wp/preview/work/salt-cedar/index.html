Salt & Cedar is a studio oyster-bar system written for a harbor room that wants brass on glass, not a clipart clam. Sit at the bar, write Oysters · Bar · Harbor, draw the SC seal, then put that badge on the dusk window, the cream list, the vinegar bottle, and the phone.

Navy is the water. Cedar is the board. Pearl is the stock. Brass is the physical sign. No food photography on the menu — the kitchen is twenty feet away. This is a studio identity, not a live account.

Full process

From the bar to the harbor

A coastal bar system written for dusk on Main Street. Sit on a stool, write the line the window can hold, draw the brass seal, then print cream, navy, and kraft until takeaway still reads as Salt & Cedar.

01 · Sit down A stool at the oyster bar

The first conversation is who walks in for a dozen, who stays for a drink, and what has to live on glass in brass — not a cartoon shell.

02 · Write it back Oysters · Bar · Harbor

The brief: navy for depth, cedar for the board, pearl for cream stock, brass for the sign. The food stays inside. The line under the seal has to agree with the menu and the bottle.

03 · Draw Brass SC, pearl ring

Official circular seal: brass SC, SALT & CEDAR on the top arc, OYSTER BAR on the bottom, wave and cedar at the sides, navy plate. Quiet serif for the list. Brass rules.

04 · Build Window, list, pack, phone

Dusk window, cream menu, vinegar and takeaway, cards, coasters, loyalty, poster, matchbook, and a homepage that uses the same navy and brass. Studio identity — Morris County, NJ.
