True Olive

The olive, not the lantern

  • Identity
    Studio study — eastern shore
  • Services
    Identity · signage · menu · site
  • Year
    2026
  • Place
    Eastern Mediterranean

The eastern shore already has limestone and oil. The mark had to hold grit and glamour: an olive in bronze, not a lantern in gold. This is not Cala’s western cove, and it is not a mosque, a crescent, or a boat sticker. The fruit is the company — the way a saw is the company when the work is wood.

The wreath is the tool. Serif TRUE is authority. Script Olive is the fruit. PRESS TABLE SHORE on the arc makes a seal. Black is only the disc. The table, the room, and the website keep every ink: tahini, limestone, olive, lemon, sumac, pomegranate, harbor, copper. This is a studio study, not a live job.

Table and site

Keep all the colors

Mediterranean restaurant sites that work put the food in full color on the first screen. Hours and the menu are HTML. The seal can be bronze; the room cannot be sanded into a black void. We studied that pattern, then built a harbor table that keeps every ink.

  1. Photography is the site

    Public tables — Zahav’s salatim and pomegranate lamb, ilili’s shared Lebanese board, Dagon’s “somewhere in the Mediterranean” — lead with the plate, not a logo on black.

  2. Inks from the produce

    Solé pulls terracotta, linen, lemon, sun-bleached stone. Levantine rooms keep tahini, sumac, olive wood, harbor. None of those inks get dropped to make a “luxury” palette of two.

  3. Reserve, hours, HTML menu

    The first jobs of the site: a visible reserve, hours you can read, a menu that is not a PDF. Then the room photograph so you know where you are sitting.

  4. Not the commodity kit

    Warm neutrals plus an overhead bowl plus decorative Arabic plus “fresh ingredients” is the fast-casual costume. True Olive refuses that. The food is specific. The colors stay.
