## What is hidden

Arithmetic.

Every table that closed did it quietly, and almost none of them did it with hatred. Hatred is loud and gets remembered. What actually happens is a count: how many plates were ordered, how many chairs fit, who was counted out before the doors opened.

Nobody has to be a villain for a person to end up outside. Someone just has to do math early, alone, and never show the work.

## Why a fortress hides it

Because capacity sounds like physics and exclusion sounds like a choice.

“We only have so many seats” ends the conversation. “I decided which seats” starts one. The list is hidden so the decision can be laundered into a constraint.

## How it surfaces

An empty chair that somebody keeps wiping.

Watch for maintenance without use — the place setting nobody sits in, the extra portion cooked out of habit, the mill-light left on for a child who has stopped coming. Grief in a kitchen looks like tidying.

## How she catches you

You walk past. Not once — as a policy.

The child at the mill, the beggar at the market, the musician with a song you almost remember. You keep moving and call it having somewhere to be. The Reader counts every WALK ON, and she does not scold. She just reads the number back to you.

> “You have places to be,” she says. “Every one of them was somewhere else.”

## Repair

Set one more place than you need. Not as charity — as **accounting**. A table that always has one spare seat cannot quietly become a list.
