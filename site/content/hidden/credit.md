## What is hidden

Credit.

A menu is a document that has already deleted somebody. It lists what arrives at the table and says nothing about the woman who stood over a pan at four in the morning learning the difference between hot and ready.

Scouts do not steal food. Food is easy. They steal **provenance** — the line that says whose hands, whose street, whose grandmother, whose accident on a bad Tuesday that became the house style.

## Why a fortress hides it

Because a name is a claim, and a claim can ask for something later.

If the bun has an auntie, the auntie has standing. If the bun is simply *product*, the only party with standing is whoever owns the sign. Hiding the cook is not cruelty for its own sake. It is **liability management**.

## How it surfaces

The mask comes off and there is a person under it, tired, holding a pan like a shield.

That is the oldest beat in the crawl and the truest: the monster is a worker protecting the thing they made. Every unmasking in WIDER is a restoration of credit.

## How she catches you

You take without asking who lost it. You accept the ring, pocket the key, lift the lantern — and never once say *whose was this?*

Taking is not the sin. **Taking without provenance** is. On the road, the Reader counts how often you chose TAKE over ASK WHO.

> “You have good hands,” she says. “You have never once asked whose recipe you are holding.”

## Repair

Ask the name. Say it out loud afterward, when the person is not in the room. That is the whole ritual. Credit is just a name repeated where it cannot be flattered into silence.
