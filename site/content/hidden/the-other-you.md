## What is hidden

The others.

You keep walking, which is allowed. But every fork leaves someone standing in it: the one who sat down at Cow Lick, the one who helped the child, the one who took the ferry even though the smile was wrong, the one who climbed the girder instead of dodging.

They do not follow you. They stay exactly where the choice happened, which is why the place feels strange when you pass through again.

## Why a fortress hides it

Motion requires it.

If you could see all of them at once you would stop, and stopping is the one thing a traveler cannot risk. So the road performs a mercy that is also a theft: it deletes the alternates so the current one can keep a straight face.

## How it surfaces

The odd grief of a familiar crossroads.

Not nostalgia — nostalgia is warm. This is colder and more specific: the sense that someone is waiting here and has been for some time, and is you, and is not angry, which is worse.

## How she catches you

She counts.

That is the entire mechanic of the tent. The Reader does not predict; she keeps a tally of the versions you abandoned and hands it back as five cards. Where you came from. What follows. What you refuse. The road ahead. What you become.

> “I keep them,” she says, tapping the deck. “Somebody has to.”

## Repair

Go back once. Not to undo it — to **acknowledge occupancy**. Sit in the fork for one minute and let the other one be real. Then walk on, with company.
