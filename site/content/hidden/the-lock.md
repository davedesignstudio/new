## What is hidden

The fear.

Ask the keep why the recipes are locked and you get a threat model: storms, thieves, competition, the scout who came sniffing last winter. All true. None of it is the reason.

The reason is that if the recipe is shared, the sharer becomes **ordinary**. The Tower is not built against Hunger. It is built against the possibility that you were never the only one who could do this.

## Why a fortress hides it

Because “I am afraid of being unnecessary” cannot be engraved over a gate.

So it becomes doctrine. Standards. Proprietary. Heritage. Every noble word for a lock is a fear wearing a coat, and the coat is load-bearing — take it off and the whole keep has to admit what it is defending.

## How it surfaces

Lightning.

A structure that cannot survive being honest will be made honest by weather. That is not a punishment; it is a property. The wards hold until the thing they protect has to be looked at, and then the sky does the looking.

The keep falls. The white stone under it was always the point.

## How she catches you

There is a door in this world with a widow beside it, and she will not use the key.

Most walkers take the key or leave the key. Almost nobody asks the door. The Reader keeps that number too — how many cycles you have gone without once asking the locked thing why it is locked.

> “All these miles,” she says, “and you have never once asked a door a question.”

## Repair

Ask the lock what it is for. Out loud, to whoever holds it, without a plan to take anything. Most locks answer immediately. They have been waiting.
