## What is hidden

The repetition.

Mars. The next colony. The clean start. The wider dark. Every one of them is sold as a frontier and most of them are a **relocation of the same table** with worse sky.

The habitat needs air, water, and a place to eat. The person who would not share bread here will not share air there. Distance is not character development.

## Why a fortress hides it

Because leaving photographs beautifully.

Nobody writes an epic about staying and doing the dishes. Departure has a soundtrack; repair does not. So the exit gets to keep its costume, and the thing being escaped travels in the hold, free of charge.

## How it surfaces

A plate in the red dust that is still warm.

Somebody cooked. Somebody is waiting to see whether you pass it. The physics of a sealed room are unforgiving about generosity — in a habitat, hoarding is measurable. Mars does not fix you; it just shortens the delay between selfishness and consequence.

## How she catches you

You turn away from crossings.

The ferryman smiles too fast, so you leave. Fair. But she watches how often *leaving* is the answer, and whether the bridges you abandon outnumber the ones you cross.

> “You are very good at going,” she says. “Tell me one thing you have arrived at.”

## Repair

Stay through one bad meal. Not a heroic stand — just the ordinary, unglamorous act of remaining at a table that is not going well. Most exits are used before the second course.
