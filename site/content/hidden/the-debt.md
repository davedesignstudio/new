## What is hidden

The account.

There is a book on the bar at Cow Lick with numbers in it, and it is a decoy. It records what can be collected. The real ledger is unwritten and much longer: who fed you when you had nothing, who covered the shift, who left the mill-light on, who did not mention it afterward.

## Why a fortress hides it

Because a house that admits its debts cannot call itself self-made.

Self-made is the most expensive story a person can buy, and the price is amnesia. You have to forget every hand, or the word stops working. So the ledger goes unwritten — not lost, just never opened.

## How it surfaces

A stranger does you a kindness and does not mention it.

That is a payment on an account you forgot you had. It will feel like luck. It is not luck; it is somebody else’s memory being better than yours.

It also surfaces the other way: as a mascot in a rubber head demanding your street. Unpaid debt does not evaporate. It gets a costume and a franchise agreement.

## How she catches you

You ask the price first.

Not always wrong — sometimes asking the price is the only defense a traveler has. But she notices the pattern: reading the book before accepting the meal, pricing the plate before sitting at it. Bookkeeping used as a way to never be indebted.

> “You always ask what it costs,” she says. “Nobody has ever been able to give you anything.”

## Repair

Accept one thing without settling it. Let it sit on the account. Being owed and owing are the same rope; a person with no debts is not free, they are **untethered**.
