## Medium first

WIDER is a **graphic novel** — book-length sequential art — not a prose novel with illustrations. The script is a blueprint for pages and panels.

- Average **3–6 panels per page**; splash pages hold wonder; dense grids speed travel
- Dialogue stays short (**~15–25 words** per balloon)
- Never say in a caption what the drawing already shows
- Use **page turns** as cliffhangers (left page tension → right page payoff)

Two production modes to choose early: **full script** (panel-by-panel) or **plot-first** (artist layouts, dialogue after).

---

## Road trip = setting, not plot

The exterior map (ports, lanes, hazards) mirrors an interior map (what each traveler must face).

1. Departure — why leave, what is left behind  
2. Route of trials — escalating stops  
3. Encounters and false friends  
4. Crisis at the furthest point  
5. Return — or refusal to return  

Each stop needs **one conflict + one character beat**. Late stops raise stakes; early stops teach the rules of the road.

---

## Space that comics can show

Build only what the camera can hold:

1. **Star geography** — every stop has one visual hook  
2. **Politics of routes** — who owns fuel, lanes, borders  
3. **Travel rules** — cost of FTL, lag, black markets  
4. **Deep history** — ruins and myths the crew inherits casually  

Scale rises from cabin → port → system → chartless dark. The galactic thread, personal thread, and mystery thread should collide by the finale.

---

## Study list (methods, not plots)

- *Understanding Comics* (McCloud) — gutters, time, transitions  
- *Saga* — intimate stakes against opera scale  
- *Descender* — odyssey emotion and visual atmosphere  
- Travel / road craft — episodic stops with a throughline  

---

## Working logline

When a debt-chased crew leaves charted space for a sealed delivery, they must keep inventing the map together — or lose each other at the edge of the wider dark.
