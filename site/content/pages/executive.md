Established by **Article II** of the Constitution, the executive branch is responsible for **enforcing and administering the laws** of the United States. It is led by the **President**, who serves as head of state, head of government, and Commander in Chief of the armed forces.

## Who makes up the executive branch

### The President

The President signs or vetoes bills passed by Congress, directs the executive departments and agencies, conducts foreign policy, negotiates treaties, and serves as Commander in Chief. The President must be a natural-born citizen, at least 35 years old, and a U.S. resident for at least 14 years.

### The Vice President

The Vice President stands first in the line of succession, serves as President of the Senate, and casts tie-breaking votes there. The 25th Amendment governs presidential succession and the filling of a vice-presidential vacancy.

### The Cabinet and federal agencies

The Cabinet includes the Vice President and the heads of the **15 executive departments** — such as State, Treasury, Defense, and Justice — who advise the President and administer their departments. Beyond the Cabinet, hundreds of federal agencies, commissions, and bureaus (from the FBI to the Environmental Protection Agency) carry out the day-to-day work of enforcing federal law.

## Key powers

- Enforce and administer federal laws
- Sign bills into law or **veto** them
- Issue executive orders and direct the federal bureaucracy
- Serve as Commander in Chief of the armed forces
- Negotiate treaties (subject to Senate ratification)
- Appoint federal judges, ambassadors, and executive officials (subject to Senate confirmation)
- Grant pardons and reprieves for federal offenses

## Checks on the other branches

The President can **veto legislation** passed by Congress and **appoint federal judges**, including Supreme Court justices, shaping the courts for generations. The Vice President's tie-breaking vote can also decide closely divided questions in the Senate.
