<p class="branch-lead">The executive branch enforces and carries out the laws passed by Congress. It is established by <strong>Article II</strong> of the Constitution and is headed by the <strong>President</strong> of the United States.</p>

## Who serves in it

- **The President** — head of state, head of government, and Commander in Chief of the armed forces, elected to a four-year term.
- **The Vice President** — first in the line of succession and President of the Senate.
- **The Cabinet** and the federal departments and agencies (State, Treasury, Defense, Justice, and more) that run the day-to-day work of government.

## What it does

- **Signs bills into law** or vetoes them.
- Issues **executive orders** and directs the federal agencies that implement and enforce the law.
- Conducts **foreign policy**, negotiates treaties, and commands the military.
- Appoints federal judges, ambassadors, and agency heads.

## Key powers it holds over the other branches

- Can **veto** legislation passed by Congress.
- **Nominates** Supreme Court justices and other federal judges.
- Can grant **pardons** and reprieves for federal offenses.
- Carries out — and can shape the enforcement of — the laws Congress writes.
