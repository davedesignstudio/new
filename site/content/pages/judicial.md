Established by **Article III** of the Constitution, the judicial branch is responsible for **interpreting the laws** of the United States and deciding whether they are consistent with the Constitution. It is headed by the **Supreme Court**, the highest court in the land.

## The federal court system

### The Supreme Court

The Supreme Court is the final arbiter of constitutional questions. Nine justices — nominated by the President and confirmed by the Senate — hear a small number of cases each year, chosen from thousands of petitions. Its decisions set binding precedent for every other court in the country.

### Courts of appeals and district courts

Congress has created two tiers of lower federal courts. The **94 district courts** are the trial courts of the federal system, where cases are first heard. The **13 courts of appeals** review decisions of the district courts within their regional circuits. Federal judges at every level are nominated by the President, confirmed by the Senate, and hold lifetime appointments to insulate them from political pressure.

## Key powers

- Interpret the meaning of federal laws and the Constitution
- **Judicial review**: strike down laws and executive actions that violate the Constitution
- Resolve disputes between states and cases involving federal law
- Set binding precedent for lower courts

## Marbury v. Madison and judicial review

The Constitution does not explicitly grant the courts the power of judicial review. Chief Justice John Marshall established it in the landmark 1803 case **_Marbury v. Madison_**, which held that &ldquo;it is emphatically the province and duty of the judicial department to say what the law is.&rdquo; From that decision forward, the courts have had the final word on whether acts of Congress and the President are constitutional.

## Checks on the other branches

The courts can declare **laws passed by Congress unconstitutional** and can invalidate **executive actions** that exceed the President's authority. In this way the judicial branch ensures that the other two branches operate within the limits set by the Constitution.
