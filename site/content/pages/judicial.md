<p class="branch-lead">The judicial branch interprets the laws and decides whether they have been applied correctly and constitutionally. It is established by <strong>Article III</strong> of the Constitution and is headed by the <strong>Supreme Court</strong> of the United States.</p>

## Who serves in it

- **The Supreme Court** — nine justices (one Chief Justice and eight Associate Justices) who serve for life during "good behavior."
- **Courts of Appeals** — thirteen circuit courts that review decisions from the district courts.
- **District Courts** — the federal trial courts where most cases begin.

## What it does

- Hears cases and **interprets the meaning of laws** and the Constitution.
- Resolves disputes between states, and between the states and the federal government.
- Applies the law to individual cases through trials and appeals.
- Protects the rights guaranteed by the Constitution.

## Key powers it holds over the other branches

- **Judicial review** — the power to strike down laws passed by Congress or actions taken by the President that it finds unconstitutional (established in *Marbury v. Madison*, 1803).
- Its lifetime appointments insulate judges from short-term political pressure.
- Interprets how the laws written by Congress and enforced by the President actually apply.
