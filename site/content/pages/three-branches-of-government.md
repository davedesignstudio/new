## The Three Branches of Government

---

The United States government is divided into three branches, a structure established by the Constitution to create a separation of powers. Each branch has its own responsibilities, and a system of checks and balances ensures that no single branch becomes too powerful.

### Legislative Branch

The legislative branch makes the laws. It is defined in Article I of the Constitution and consists of Congress, which is made up of two chambers: the Senate and the House of Representatives. Congress also has the power to declare war, approve the federal budget, confirm presidential appointments, and ratify treaties.

### Executive Branch

The executive branch carries out and enforces the laws. It is defined in Article II of the Constitution and is headed by the President, who also serves as commander-in-chief of the armed forces. The executive branch includes the Vice President, the Cabinet, and federal agencies and departments.

### Judicial Branch

The judicial branch interprets the laws. It is defined in Article III of the Constitution and consists of the Supreme Court and the lower federal courts. The courts evaluate laws and can strike down those that conflict with the Constitution, a power known as judicial review.

### Checks and Balances

Each branch can limit the powers of the others. For example, the President can veto legislation passed by Congress, Congress can override a veto and confirm or reject judicial appointments, and the courts can declare laws or executive actions unconstitutional. This balance protects against the concentration of power in any one branch.
