## The Separation of Powers

The United States Constitution divides the federal government into three branches so that no single person or group holds too much power. Each branch has its own distinct responsibilities, and each can limit the powers of the others through a system of **checks and balances**.

---

| Branch | Main Role | Who |
|--------|-----------|-----|
| Legislative | Makes the laws | Congress (Senate & House of Representatives) |
| Executive | Enforces the laws | President, Vice President & the Cabinet |
| Judicial | Interprets the laws | Supreme Court & the federal courts |

---

### 1. The Legislative Branch

The legislative branch is established by **Article I** of the Constitution. It consists of the two chambers of Congress:

- **The Senate** — 100 members, two from each state, serving six-year terms.
- **The House of Representatives** — 435 members, apportioned by state population, serving two-year terms.

Congress drafts and votes on proposed laws, declares war, regulates interstate and foreign commerce, and controls taxing and spending.

### 2. The Executive Branch

The executive branch is established by **Article II** of the Constitution. It is led by the **President**, who serves as head of state, head of government, and Commander-in-Chief of the armed forces.

- The **Vice President** supports the President and presides over the Senate.
- The **Cabinet** — the heads of the 15 executive departments — advises the President on matters such as defense, treasury, and foreign affairs.

This branch signs bills into law (or vetoes them), negotiates treaties, and enforces the laws Congress passes.

### 3. The Judicial Branch

The judicial branch is established by **Article III** of the Constitution. It is headed by the **Supreme Court**, made up of nine justices who are nominated by the President and confirmed by the Senate.

The federal courts interpret the meaning of laws, decide how they apply to individual cases, and can strike down laws that violate the Constitution through the power of **judicial review**.

---

## Checks and Balances

Each branch can restrain the others. For example:

- The President can **veto** legislation passed by Congress.
- Congress can **override a veto** with a two-thirds vote and can **impeach** the President or federal judges.
- The Supreme Court can declare laws or executive actions **unconstitutional**.

This balance of power protects liberty by keeping any one branch from dominating the government.
