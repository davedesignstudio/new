<p class="branch-lead">No single branch is meant to dominate the others. The Constitution gives each branch specific powers to limit — or <em>check</em> — the other two, keeping power balanced across the whole system.</p>

## The Legislative branch can…

- Override a presidential **veto** with a two-thirds vote of both chambers.
- **Impeach and remove** the President and federal judges.
- Confirm or reject the President's appointments and **ratify treaties** (Senate).
- Control funding for the executive branch and create or eliminate lower federal courts.

## The Executive branch can…

- **Veto** bills passed by Congress.
- **Nominate** federal judges and Supreme Court justices.
- Issue **pardons** for federal crimes.
- Call special sessions of Congress and shape how laws are enforced.

## The Judicial branch can…

- Declare acts of Congress **unconstitutional** through judicial review.
- Declare actions of the President **unconstitutional**.
- Interpret how laws and treaties apply to real cases.

## Why it matters

The framers of the Constitution had lived under a government where power was concentrated in one place. By **separating powers** into three branches and letting each one check the others, they built a system designed to prevent any person or group from gaining unchecked control.
