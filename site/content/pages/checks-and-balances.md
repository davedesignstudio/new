The framers of the Constitution feared concentrated power, so they gave each branch ways to restrain the other two. This system of **checks and balances** ensures that the legislative, executive, and judicial branches remain coequal — no branch can act entirely on its own.

> &ldquo;Ambition must be made to counteract ambition.&rdquo;
>
> — James Madison, *Federalist No. 51*

## How each branch checks the others

| Branch | Checks on the other branches |
| ------ | ---------------------------- |
| **Legislative** | Overrides presidential vetoes with a two-thirds vote; confirms or rejects appointments; ratifies treaties; controls federal funding; impeaches and removes the President and federal judges; proposes constitutional amendments. |
| **Executive** | Vetoes bills passed by Congress; nominates federal judges and Supreme Court justices; grants pardons; directs how laws are implemented. |
| **Judicial** | Declares acts of Congress unconstitutional; invalidates executive actions that exceed presidential authority; sets precedent that shapes future law. |

## Checks and balances in action

### The veto and the override

When Congress passes a bill, the President may **veto** it — but the veto is not final. If two-thirds of both the House and the Senate vote to **override**, the bill becomes law without the President's signature.

### Appointments and confirmation

The President nominates Cabinet secretaries, ambassadors, and federal judges, but the Senate must **confirm** them. A President who wants a judge on the bench needs the legislative branch to agree.

### Judicial review

Even after a bill survives Congress and the President, the courts may still strike it down. Through **judicial review**, established in *Marbury v. Madison* (1803), judges can declare laws and executive actions unconstitutional.

### Impeachment

Congress can remove the President, federal judges, and other civil officers for &ldquo;Treason, Bribery, or other high Crimes and Misdemeanors.&rdquo; The House **impeaches** by majority vote, and the Senate holds the **trial**, with a two-thirds vote required for removal.

### Amending the Constitution

When the Supreme Court interprets the Constitution, its word is usually final — but not always. Congress and the states can respond by **amending the Constitution** itself, as they did when the 16th Amendment authorized a federal income tax after the Court had struck one down.

## Why it matters

Checks and balances slow government down by design. Laws must survive passage in two chambers, a presidential signature, and possible court review. The system trades speed for stability, protecting liberty by making sure that power is never wielded by one branch — or one person — alone.
