## The Three Branches of Government

The United States federal government is divided into three separate branches. This separation of powers ensures that no single branch becomes too powerful, with each branch able to check and balance the others.

---

### Legislative Branch

The legislative branch makes the laws. It is made up of Congress, which is split into two chambers: the **Senate** and the **House of Representatives**. Together they draft, debate, and pass legislation, control federal spending, and declare war.

### Executive Branch

The executive branch enforces the laws. It is headed by the **President**, who serves as head of state, head of government, and commander-in-chief of the armed forces. The **Vice President** and the President's Cabinet support this branch in carrying out and administering federal law.

### Judicial Branch

The judicial branch interprets the laws. It is led by the **Supreme Court** along with the lower federal courts. These courts evaluate laws, resolve disputes, and determine whether laws are consistent with the Constitution.

---

Together, these three branches keep one another in balance through a system of **checks and balances**, a cornerstone of constitutional government.
