Nobody can list every book in every library on Earth. That is the first true sentence. Anyone who hands you a complete catalog is selling a spear and calling it a bag.

A **holding** is a copy on a shelf, or a record that says a copy is there. A **title** is the work those copies are trying to be. A **mouth** is the story a person actually tells. The three are not the same. Callimachus already knew: the Pinakes named a person, then a first line, then a doubt. Google, WorldCat, and the national stacks are still doing that work. They have not finished. They will not finish. Sunday’s number is already wrong.

This page is not the books. It is the *sillybos* tied to the idea of the books. The one library we sat with at length is [Alexandria](/alexandria). The named storehouses through time are [Stacks](/stacks). The talk with other tellers is [Libraries](/libraries).

---

## What you are even counting

Papyrus roll. Clay tablet. Codex. Woodblock. Palm leaf. Scroll sealed in a cave. A talking book. A file. A serial that pretends to be one book and is a thousand. Lecture notes bound as a series. The same novel cataloged five times because five libraries used five rules.

Google’s book-search engineers said this out loud in 2010, after clustering a billion raw records. Once they kept only things printed and bound, and tried to squash duplicates, they arrived at **129,864,880** books. They dated the joke: *at least until Sunday.*

That number is a method, not a census. It skipped serials. It could not see every language equally. It could not see a manuscript that never went to a publisher. It could not see a book that burned. UNESCO’s order-of-magnitude for new titles each year is a couple of million. Add those years to 2010 and you get a larger guess. A guess is not every book.

ISBN arrived in the 1970s. Whole centuries of writing never wore one. Self-published files wear too many. Legal deposit tries to catch “every book of this country.” It still misses the stall that never mailed a copy, the language with no agency, the cook who wrote the recipe on the wall.

## The union catalog

The closest living Pinakes for “what libraries say they hold” is **WorldCat**, begun in 1971 when Ohio University added the first records to OCLC’s online union catalog.

As of October 2025, WorldCat reported:

| What they count | How many | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bibliographic records | 609,755,407 | Descriptions. One record can stand for a series, a set, or a single volume. |
| Holdings | 3,581,565,435 | Copies (and copy-claims) in participating libraries. A popular title is many holdings. A rare title may be one. |
| Languages | 488 (Nov 2025) | About 61% of items are not in English. |
| Knowledge-base e-records | 96,081,922 (Sep 2025) | Licensed and open electronic collections, which are not the same as a bound book. |

WorldCat gets more than one new record every second. That is the sound of the catalog admitting it is behind.

A holding is not a title. Three and a half billion holdings are not three and a half billion books. They are how many times libraries have raised a hand and said *we have this, or we have a record of this.* Many libraries on Earth are not in WorldCat. School rooms, parish cupboards, monastery chests, phone-scans, private walls. The union catalog is the largest table we have. It is not the Earth.

## National stacks that try to keep “every book of here”

Legal deposit is the modern harbor rule with better manners: the country asks for a copy of what is published under its sky, and (in decent versions) does not keep your original and send you a fake.

These are **items**, not unique novels. Maps, recordings, manuscripts, patents, stamps, and websites sit in the same boast.

| Storehouse | What they say they hold | The catch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/) | More than **181 million** physical items (FY2024), plus vast digital collections. Started in 1800 with 152 titles. Burned in 1814; Jefferson’s library restocked it; another fire in 1851. | Largest comprehensive library by many counts. Still not every book on Earth. |
| [British Library](https://www.bl.uk/) | **200 million+** items; on the order of **14 million** printed books and e-books; legal deposit for the UK and Ireland. | A copy of every UK/Irish publication is the aim, not the world’s backlist. |
| [Bibliothèque nationale de France](https://www.bnf.fr/) | Legal deposit since the *ordonnance* of 1537. | One of the oldest continuous “every book of here” laws. Still a country, not a planet. |
| [National Library of China](https://www.nlc.cn/) | Tens of millions of items; among the richest Chinese collections. | A civilization’s stack is not a union of all stacks. |
| [Bibliotheca Alexandrina](https://www.bibalex.org/) | A new storehouse on the old water. | Not the Mouseion. A reminder. |

No national library holds every book on Earth. That was the Ptolemaic hunger. It remains a hunger.

## What the count cannot hold

- **The burned unique.** Maya codices at Maní. Nalanda’s palm leaves. A Warsaw vault in 1944. A Timbuktu chest that did not make the cart. One copy, then none.
- **The sealed cache.** Dunhuang Cave 17. Nag Hammadi. Qumran. The Cairo Geniza — discarded writing stored because holy letters must not be thrown in the street. A geniza is a library that thought it was a grave, and so it lived.
- **The carbonized private stack.** The Villa of the Papyri at Herculaneum: the only Greco-Roman library that still exists as a room of rolls, unreadable until someone invents a kinder light.
- **The oral.** A story that was never a book. Griots, winter tellers, a grandmother’s bun. Morrison: the room performs it together. If you only count bindings, you have already buried the supper.
- **The uncatalogued.** A kitchen shelf. A van glovebox. A phone. Road-Wisdom’s five cards. The Paperback’s fifty-two sections are a book you walk, not a WorldCat record.

Callimachus listed doubts about ascription. The honest catalog of Earth still needs that column.

## What we took

**Store, don’t bury.** [The Hidden Parts](/hidden) keep what you would not sit with. That is closer to a geniza than to a fortress.

**A holding is not a meaning.** WorldCat can say a library holds *the Iliad*. It cannot say which city-tag the roll wore — Chios, Sinope, Massalia — or which traveler you were when you read it. The Reader names a pattern from how you walked. She does not claim 609 million records.

**Sunday’s number.** Google dated its count. We date ours. October 2025 for WorldCat. FY2024 for the Library of Congress. The road will need a new scrap.

**Compare mouths.** Many Homers become a Homer you can share. Many catalogs become a union catalog. The work is collation, not a miracle of completeness.

## What we refused

- A dump of titles pretending to be every book. That is soup. It is also a lie.
- A number big enough to sound like supper. 129 million and 609 million and 3.5 billion are all true in their own units. None of them is the Earth.
- The harbor rule. We do not confiscate your original and hand you a copy.
- Fortune as a complete library of the soul. The Mouseion did not have that catalog. Neither does WorldCat. Neither does she.
