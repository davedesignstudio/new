## A table between <em>three worlds</em>

---

Sattva began with a simple question: what happens when the quiet of a Stockholm archipelago kitchen, the stillness of a Kyoto tea room, and the devotion of a Varanasi household altar meet at one table?

From **Sweden** we took *lagom* — the art of just enough. Birch, oak, linen, and light; food measured by season rather than by abundance.

From **Japan** we took *ma* (間) — the space between things. An empty part of the plate is not missing anything; it is where the eye rests and the palate breathes.

From the **Vedic tradition** we took *sattva* (सत्त्व) — purity of essence. Every dish is prepared as an offering, guided by the old blessing *Atithi Devo Bhava*: the guest is divine.

The room holds twelve seats along a single oak counter. The menu changes with the moon and the market. We ask only that you arrive unhurried.
