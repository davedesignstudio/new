**We help independent restaurants look as exceptional as the food they serve.**

That is the first true sentence of the studio. The plate is already honest. The room, the menu, the card, the sign, the night — those should tell the same story. We do not armor a kitchen into a fortress. We set a seat that looks like the food.

WIDER is the long table we built to prove it: a playable comic told by **Zorya**, a fictional old roadside reader. She stories together a prairie table, a traveling kettle, and a winter stove as **one supper** — not three captions stacked on the same panel.

The plot is simple on purpose. A kitchen tries to be a fortress. Hunger knocks the walls down. The survivors eat. The van full of hungry fools was always driving to that table. The Game Boy road is you, walking the same Earth while she watches.

Food is the clue, not the garnish: who cooked, who was left out, who tried to franchise the bun.

Start with [the story](/story). What she remembers, told as one supper, is [History](/history). The old stories’ load-bearing stone is [Keystone](/keystone). Drive it in [The Paperback](/adventure). Sit at [the table](/table) — she is the DM, and every night is rolled on the fly. The six kitchens are on [the route](/route). What Earth stores instead of burying is in [The Hidden Parts](/hidden). The talk with other tellers is [Libraries](/libraries). Named storehouses through time: [Stacks](/stacks). The count of books, which cannot finish: [Holdings](/holdings). The Mouseion: [Alexandria](/alexandria). The old mouths in English: [Mouths](/mouths).

If you run a house and the room is lagging the plate, [sit with the studio](/contact).
