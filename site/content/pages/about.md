## Separation of powers

When the United States Constitution was written in 1787, its authors deliberately divided the federal government into three separate branches, each with its own responsibilities:

- The **Legislative branch** (Congress) makes the laws.
- The **Executive branch** (the President) carries out the laws.
- The **Judicial branch** (the courts) interprets the laws.

This division is called the **separation of powers**. Each branch is given tools to limit the power of the other two — a system known as **checks and balances** — so that no single branch can become too powerful.
