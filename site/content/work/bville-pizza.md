Bville already had the food. The brand was a pile of one-off files — a logo that did not match the menu, a menu that did not match the site. Guests were doing the work the identity should have done.

The job was to put every surface in one field. Wordmark, category type, and photo crop repeat so the shop reads as itself whether you are on the sidewalk or on your phone. That is **similarity**: the brand does not reinvent its shape at each touchpoint.

The menu was rebuilt with **proximity**. Pies, grinders, and plates sit in tight groups. Prices lock to the dish they belong to. The eye does not cross empty space to finish a thought.

The site carries the same hierarchy. No template retrofit. The restaurant keeps its own layout, because the next shop down the street should not look like this one.
