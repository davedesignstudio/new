This is the working character bible for WIDER. It is research turned into decisions: how the cast grows without becoming six copies of the same hunger.

---

## What the research agrees on

### Want vs need

Craft consensus (screenwriting and novel craft alike): the **want** is the visible plot goal. The **need** is the inner change the character resists. The want drives scenes. The need is what actually heals them. If two characters share the same want, you get conflict. If two share the same need, you get redundancy — cut or differentiate.

Applied rule for WIDER: every major seat gets a distinct need. Shared wants (the Castle Nova slider, a seat at the table) are allowed. Shared needs are not.

### Ensemble balance

Ensembles fail when one lead owns every arc and the rest react, or when focus rotates so fast nobody lands. Fix: map wants and arcs first; stagger emotional peaks; give meaningful time, not equal time. Readers track about five or six mains before investment thins. WIDER’s six: **Zorya, Bodie, Klax, Grub, VANESSA, Prophet Gary**. Guests (auntie, widow, princess, Bind) echo the theme and leave.

### Buddy / road-trip foils

Buddy comedy runs on contrast under a shared exterior goal. Friction is the chemistry. The road is a pressure cooker: remove home, force detours, make apology a skill. Structure often includes a mid-journey “breakup,” then a reunion that feels earned. For Bodie/Klax: same destination (slider / survival), opposite operating systems (confidence vs itinerary). The van is the room they cannot leave.

### Mentors who are people

A mentor embodies the story’s thematic truth. They must not solve the climax. Best mentors are flawed, sometimes wrong, sometimes withholding for a reason. The Sage teaches by question and parable, not lecture. Zorya deals cards; she does not write the guests’ names for them. Her restraint is the craft.

### Comic relief that survives deletion

If removing the funny character does not change the plot, they are decoration. Humor should come from personality under pressure. Grub, VANESSA, and Gary each change outcomes: unmasking, transport/refusal, prophecy interrupted into truth.

## Theme each arc serves

**Hunger that will not share vs a table that will.**

Every arc answers that. Fortress fails. Guests who pass the ketchup live. The teller who only sells futures is a liar; the teller who seats fate is honest.

## Relationship map (who changes whom)

- **Bodie ↔ Klax:** foil partners. Mid-story break when confidence and itinerary both fail the diner. Reunion is passing the ketchup — commensality as friendship.
- **Grub → party:** courage catalyst. He unmasks when the boys freeze. His need beat is one scene without a treat.
- **VANESSA → boys:** mother/vehicle. She refuses to start until they share. Her need beat is being thanked without a punchline.
- **Gary → theme:** false structure. His prophecy is the franchise map; Zorya steals his lines and seats the hound.
- **Zorya → everyone:** thematic truth. She does not finish their arcs. She names the pattern after they walk it.

## Staggered Story Circles (macro)

Dan Harmon’s circle (You → Need → Go → Search → Find → Take → Return → Change) applied so peaks do not collide:

1. **Act I — Couch / Door:** Bodie & Klax *Go*. Gary *announces*. VANESSA *starts on the third try*.
2. **Act I–II — Mart / Runes:** Klax’s plan burns. Bodie’s confidence spikes wrong. Grub gets the Gem (*Find* for Grub).
3. **Act II — Market / Auntie:** Theme lands (culture vs franchise). Klax listens. Bodie sits.
4. **Act II low — Diner quarrel:** Buddy breakup. VANESSA stalls. Need pressure high.
5. **Act II–III — Carnival / Sphinx:** Grub or Grub Gem solves mask. Gary’s prophecy fails mid-caption.
6. **Act III — Tower → Star / Service:** Zorya’s truth visible. Boys *Return* changed (they can say the quiet thing). Zorya’s quiet need: someone sat; she continues.

Road-Wisdom loop is Zorya’s infinite B-plot: the seeker’s traits accumulate; her greeting changes; she never GAME OVERs.

## Voice rules (so they never sound alike)

- **Zorya:** short parable + one concrete kitchen image. Never slang from the boys. Never a caricature accent.
- **Bodie:** short sentences. Destiny words. Softens when hungry or wrong.
- **Klax:** lists, caveats, “technically.” Softens when the list fails and he laughs once.
- **Grub:** food first. One brave silence per arc is louder than puns.
- **VANESSA:** glyphs, delayed verbs, maternal threats. No face — lights only.
- **Gary:** unbroken majestic diction until interrupted. The interruption is the joke and the thesis.

---

## Anti-patterns we refuse

- Equal page time for its own sake.
- Identical “learn to trust” needs on Bodie and Klax.
- Zorya as omniscient plot solver.
- Grub as only a punchline.
- Stereotyped “mystic” framing of Zorya’s people — she is a fictional traveling reader; fortune is craft and hospitality, not ethnicity-as-magic.
- Princess as Disney rescue-only; she is Tower residue.
