## Separation is not enough

If Congress only made law, the President only executed it, and judges only judged, a determined majority in one branch could still run away with the government. So the Constitution mixes functions on purpose. The President takes part in legislation through the veto. The Senate takes part in execution through confirmation. Judges take part in both when they refuse to apply an unconstitutional act.

The branches are separate institutions that share powers. That is the American design, not three sealed boxes.

## Why the Framers feared “the accumulation of powers”

Federalist No. 47 quotes Montesquieu: there can be no liberty if the same man, or the same body, makes the law and executes it. The American answer was not to weaken government until it could do nothing. It was to give each department “the necessary constitutional means, and personal motives, to resist encroachments of the others.”

That is why terms differ (two, four, and six years; and life). Why constituencies differ (districts, a national electoral coalition, and no constituency at all). Why each branch is paid from a different political clock.

## When the system strains

Checks fail when one branch declines to use them, or when the others cannot. A Congress that will not oversee; a President who will not execute a statute; a Court that is ignored — each is a constitutional problem the text cannot solve by itself. The document assumes officers who want their own power more than they want another branch’s victory.

It also assumes voters. Elections are the check that sits outside the triangle: the House every two years, the President every four, the Senate in rolling thirds. Judges are deliberately excused from that rhythm.

## How to read a headline

When a modern fight is described as “constitutional crisis,” ask three quieter questions first:

1. Which clause is actually in play — veto, purse, appointment, war, review?
2. Does the unhappy branch have a tool it has not used?
3. Is the dispute about the meaning of a power, or about the will to use it?

Most collisions are the system working loudly. A true crisis is when a branch’s tool is gone, or no longer feared.
