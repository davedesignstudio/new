Why so few make it

Most bills die quietly

Thousands of bills are introduced in a two-year Congress and only a few hundred become law. Almost all of them are never voted on at all — they are referred to a committee that simply never takes them up.

That is the design working as intended. A proposal that cannot hold a majority in two chambers and survive a presidential signature was, in the framers' view, not ready to bind the whole country.

Three ways a bill becomes law

  • Signature. The President signs it.
  • Inaction. Ten days pass while Congress is in session and the President has not signed or vetoed it.
  • Override. Two-thirds of both chambers vote to enact it over a veto.

There is also a fourth outcome: a pocket veto, when Congress adjourns during those ten days and the unsigned bill simply dies.
