Ambition must be made to counteract ambition. The interest of the man must be connected with the constitutional rights of the place.

James Madison, Federalist No. 51, 1788

Separation is not isolation

The branches were never meant to run in parallel lanes. Madison's design deliberately overlaps them, so that each one needs the cooperation of another to accomplish anything permanent.

The checks run both ways

Every branch is checked by the other two. The President can veto a bill, but Congress can override the veto, and the courts can void the resulting law. No arrow in this system points only outward.

Elections are a check too

The House stands for reelection every two years, a third of the Senate every two, and the President every four. Voters get the final and most frequent check of all.
