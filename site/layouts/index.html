The Three Branches of the U.S. Government

The Constitution divides federal power among three coequal branches — legislative, executive, and judicial — so that no single branch can grow too powerful. One makes the laws, one enforces them, and one interprets them.

Article I

Legislative

Makes the law

Congress — the Senate and the House of Representatives — writes, debates, and passes federal legislation.

Explore the branch →

Article II

Executive

Enforces the law

The President, Vice President, Cabinet, and federal agencies carry out and administer the nation's laws.

Explore the branch →

Article III

Judicial

Interprets the law

The Supreme Court and the lower federal courts interpret laws and decide whether they square with the Constitution.

Explore the branch →

How a law travels through the branches

  1. Congress passes a bill.
  2. The President signs it into law — or vetoes it.
  3. The courts may review whether the law is constitutional.

“Ambition must be made to counteract ambition.”

— James Madison, Federalist No. 51
