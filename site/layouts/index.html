United States Government

Three Branches, One Balance of Power

The U.S. Constitution divides the federal government into three co-equal branches. Each one has a distinct job, and each one can check the power of the others.

No branch stands above the others. Through a system of checks and balances, each branch can limit the power of the other two — so that power stays shared, not concentrated.
