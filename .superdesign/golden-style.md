# House style after William Golden — “Stamped Seal”

Researched September 2026: William Golden (CBS, 1937–1959), the Eye (1950–51, drawn by Kurt Weihs from a Shaker “all-seeing eye” in Alexey Brodovitch’s Portfolio), Didot as the house serif.

## Golden principles we keep

1. **Simple order out of many elements.** One container, one symbol, two lines of type. Nothing decorative.
2. **Respect the stubborn facts.** The room, the town, and the food fix the mark — a fox for a hunt inn, a knocker for a supper door, an apple for a press.
3. **Distinguished, not decorated.** High-contrast serif (Playfair stands in for Didot), one script accent (Allura), one grotesque for service copy (Montserrat).
4. **Proportion first.** Double gold rings (54/40 rhythm like the Eye’s outer/inner circles), generous negative space that can hold a photo.
5. **A system that repeats.** The same seal must work on a visiting card, a shirt, a cap, a truck door, a hanging sign, a leather apron, a window, and a 16px favicon. Golden’s rule via Stanton: do not abandon the mark when you get bored — that is when guests start noticing it.

## Our translation

- Circular double-ring gold seal on maroon/navy, raised-foil treatment from the studio card.
- Rim copy as the menu promise (Hunt · Table · Fire / Press · Table · Season).
- Print lockup on matte black with phone; window gold leaf on millwork.
- Live sites in the same navy/gold type system (`gold-house.css`).
